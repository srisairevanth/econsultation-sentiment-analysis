Dodd-Frank Wall Street Reform and Consumer Protection Act (Overview)

The Dodd-Frank Act was enacted in 2010 in response to the 2008 financial crisis. Its stated
goals include promoting financial stability, ending "too big to fail," protecting consumers
from abusive financial practices, and increasing transparency in complex financial markets.

Key components commonly referenced in public comments include:
- Creation of the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB) to supervise consumer financial
  products and services.
- The "Volcker Rule," which restricts banks from certain kinds of speculative proprietary trading.
- Enhanced regulatory oversight of large, systemically important financial institutions.
- New requirements for derivatives markets, including central clearing for many swaps.
- Whistleblower protections and incentive programs for reporting securities law violations.
